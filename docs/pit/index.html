Carolina BarbecueThe pit (11)

Cuts, fuel, pits, tools and methods.

Cookers (2)

gas and electric cookers

gas pit, gas cooker, electric cooker

The cookers most Carolina barbecue is cooked in: steel ovens fired by propane, natural gas or an electric element, with a thermostat and racks, and often a box for wood chips or a log to put smoke on the meat.

pig cooker

pig roaster, hog cooker, trailer cooker

A steel box on a trailer chassis, long enough for a split hog, with a hinged lid, a grate, and either a row of propane burners underneath or a bed for charcoal: the cooker of the pig pickin', towed to the church lot…

whole hog

whole-hog barbecue, whole hog, a pig

The entire dressed pig — dressed meaning gutted and scraped of hair, the head sometimes left on — split open along the backbone, laid flat over coals and cooked as one piece, then pulled from the bones and chopped…

Fuel (1)

hickory and oak

hickory, oak, hardwood

The hardwoods of the Carolina pit. Hickory and oak are the woods a Carolina cook burns, usually together and usually burned down to coals before they go under the meat: Lexington pits cook over oak and hickory coals…

Methods (1)

direct heat

coals under the meat, open-pit, pit-cooked

The Carolina way of putting fire to meat: a bed of hardwood coals directly beneath the hog or the shoulders, with no flame and no separate firebox.

brick pit

Lexington pit, closed pit, pit house

The masonry pit of the Piedmont: a closed brick chamber with a rack for the shoulders and a chimney through the roof to carry off the smoke.

Preparation (1)

hog killing

hog killin', hog-killing day, killing hogs

The winter slaughter of the farm's hogs, done on the first cold day of autumn or winter, when the meat would keep.
