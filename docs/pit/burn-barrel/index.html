the pitburn barrel

also: burn-barrel · coal barrel · fire barrel · the barrel · burner

A repurposed steel drum — or, at Scott's in Hemingway, two 55-gallon drums welded together — in which logs are burned down to coals: the wood goes in at the top, the coals are pulled out from below and carried by shovel to the pit. It is the coal-maker of the wood pit, the reason a whole hog can sit over a steady bed of coals for twelve hours with no flame under it. Older pit houses use a fireplace for the same job — the Skylight Inn burns its oak and hickory in fireplaces beside the pits — and the barrel is the version anyone can weld.

The story Cited · web-npr-scott-pit-burned

The barrel is the modern form of an older arrangement. Tradition holds — the trench pit of the farm barbecue had a fire burning at one end and coals raked down its length under the meat; when the pit climbed above ground into blocks and brick, the fire moved into a fireplace or a drum beside it. Mallard Creek Presbyterian Church in Charlotte cooked its first barbecues, from 1929, in ditches with hickory coals shoved underneath the whole hogs. At Scott's Bar-B-Que in Hemingway, Rodney Scott feeds hunks of hickory, oak and pecan through the top of the welded drums and pulls hot coals from below to stoke the pit. In November 2013 the family's wooden cookhouse caught fire — 'shooting like a torch', the fire department told him — and Scott took his hogs on the road to raise at least $100,000, part to rebuild and part for a fund to preserve the region's food traditions.

How it is done Tradition

Split logs go in at the top and burn with the draft from the bottom; as they collapse into coals the cook rakes them out with a flat shovel and spreads them in the pit under the meat. Tradition holds — the bed goes heavier under the hams and shoulders and thinner under the loin, and a hog cooked through a night takes a fresh shovelful on a regular round, so the barrel is kept fed the whole time. Lexington crews call the same round, in a brick pit, 'firing the pits'.

Today Cited · web-uncpress-reed-myth

Every wood-fired whole-hog pit keeps a barrel, a fireplace or a burner going through the night; the drum is the standard beside a block pit and at Scott's. Gas and electric cookers do without it, and the labor it stands for is the reason owners give for switching.
