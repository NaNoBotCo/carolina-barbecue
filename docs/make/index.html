At most whole-hog pits the meat goes on with salt and little else, and the seasoning that decides the flavour happens after the cook: the meat is chopped on the block and dressed there with vinegar sauce, and at the Skylight Inn with Texas Pete besides. A heavy rub is a Memphis and Texas habit that Carolina pits mostly did not take up. Start light. Season at the block.

Amounts per pound are this project's rule of thumb, not a pit's measurement — the proportions inside each level are the cited thing. A rub is easy to overdo and hard to undo; the block will take care of the rest.

Chop finer than instinct says. Eastern pits mince it near as small as the meat so the two marry on the bun; Lexington pits chop fine and stain it with the dip. Dress ahead, chill until it weeps.
