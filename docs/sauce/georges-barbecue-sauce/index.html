a sauceGeorge's Barbecue Sauce

also: George's Sauce · George's Original · George's of Nashville

George's, on shelves across eastern North Carolina; the label says George's Barbecue Sauce.

A bottled sauce from Nashville, North Carolina, a Nash County town on the edge of the Coastal Plain, sold in three strengths: Original, Hot and Special. The Original's label reads vinegar, water, tomato concentrate, tomato paste, sugar, apples, salt, high fructose corn syrup, corn syrup, red pepper and spices — vinegar leading, tomato third and fourth, and a sweetening that runs to three separate entries. It carries 3.5 grams of sugar in a tablespoon and 180 milligrams of sodium. The Special reverses the first four: tomato concentrate and tomato paste come before the water and the vinegar, and jalapenos join the list.

The story Cited · web-georges-our-story

The company dates itself to 1975 and names George Stallings as its founder, who worked the recipe out in his outdoor garage; after it caught on at home, he and his brother cooked, bottled and sold it by hand. The maker's own page says the sauces are still made by hand in small batches to that recipe, and that 4,500 bottles a day are poured, packed and shipped by hand from Womble Road in Nashville.

Inference — the label is worth reading against the map. Nashville sits in the eastern half of the state, where the sauce that goes on a chopped hog is vinegar and pepper with no tomato in it. George's pours vinegar first, then puts tomato third — the order of the Piedmont dip, not the Coastal Plain's — and sells it as an eastern-style sauce. A bottle that has to keep on a shelf and please a wide market is not the same object as a sauce mixed in a bucket behind a pit, and the reading here describes the bottle.

How it is done Cited · web-georges-our-story

Poured over chopped or pulled pork, brushed on chicken and ribs, used as a mop while a shoulder cooks. It is thin enough to soak rather than coat. Three strengths sit beside each other on the same shelf: Original, Hot, and Special, which is the tomato-first one with jalapenos.

Today Cited · web-georges-our-story

Made and shipped from Nashville, North Carolina. The three bottles are sold from the company's site and through eastern North Carolina grocers.

Eastern North Carolina Barbecue Sauce

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • ¾ gallons apple cider vinegar
  • ⅛ cup salt
  • ¼ cup ground black pepper
  • ¼ cup red pepper flakes
  • 1 cup molasses
  • 2 cups honey
1. Start with apple cider vinegar in saucepan over medium heat. 2. Add salt, ground black pepper, crushed red pepper, molasses, and honey. 3. Let mixture heat up, but not quite boil. 4. Mix well. 5. Return sauce to the jug that the vinegar came in to cool.

Makes ¾ gallon.

Vinegar first, as on this label. The maker's own formula is not published; these are free-to-use recipes of the same family, for a cook who wants to make a jar.

Sauce for Barbecues (receipt 335)

Mrs. A. P. Hill · Mrs. Hill's New Cook-Book · 1867 · p. 171 · source · Public domain
Melt half a pound of butter; stir into it a large tablespoonful of mustard, half a teaspoonful of red pepper, one of black, salt to taste; add vinegar until the sauce has a strong acid taste. The quantity of vinegar will depend upon the strength of it. As soon as the meat becomes hot, begin to baste, and continue basting frequently until it is done; pour over the meat any sauce that remains.

Vinegar, salt and both peppers, with butter and mustard: the Southern mop of 1867.

Barbecue Sauce (Sweet)

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • 1 cup tomato catsup (ketchup)
  • ¾ cup cider vinegar
  • 2–3 tablespoons mustard
  • 3 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 1–2 teaspoons celery seed
  • 3 tablespoons margarine
  • ¼ teaspoon black pepper
  • ¼ teaspoon red pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon onion flakes
  • Garlic powder or minced garlic to taste (optional)
1. Combine all ingredients together and bring to a boil. Reduce heat and simmer 10 to 15 minutes, until it thickens slightly. 2. Arrange chicken pieces or pork chops in a greased baking dish. 3. Pour half of sauce over and bake at 350°F (175°C) for 30 to 40 minutes. 4. Pour remaining sauce over before serving.

Cider vinegar and ketchup in the other proportion, for the sweeter end of the range.

Not to be confused with

  • Scott's Barbecue Sauce — Both are eastern North Carolina bottles. Scott's label names four ingredients and no sugar; George's names ten, of which three are sweeteners.
