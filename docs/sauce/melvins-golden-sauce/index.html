a sauceMelvin's Golden Sauce

also: Melvin's Original Golden Secret · Golden Secret Sauce

The Golden, at Melvin's counters in Charleston and Mount Pleasant.

The mustard sauce of Melvin's Bar-B-Que in Charleston, sold in 16-ounce glass bottles, half gallons and gallons, beside the house's Southern Red, Sweet Red and Vinegar sauces. The maker publishes no ingredient list and no Nutrition Facts for any of the four, so what this page can give is the history the family tells and the shape of its line, not a measurement.

The story Cited · web-melvinsbbq-golden

The maker's account dates the sauce to 1933 and to Melvin Bessinger's father, Joe Bessinger — Big Joe on the bottle's copy — and says the recipe passed down spoken and was never written. Joe Bessinger's mustard sauce at Holly Hill is the same sauce the mustard record carries as the Midlands origin story, and his sons carried it out of the Midlands in four directions: Maurice to West Columbia, Melvin and Thomas to Charleston, Robert to Charleston too.

The present line is four sauces and reads as a map of the whole subject. Golden is the family mustard. Southern Red the house calls a tangy tomato sauce blending vinegar and tomato. Sweet Red the current owner describes as a Kansas City style he worked out in his own kitchen. Vinegar, the newest, the house says its pitmaster made for the owner's wife, who grew up in North Carolina on vinegar barbecue. Inference — a Charleston counter selling all four is not unusual now; what is unusual is that it sells them as four named things rather than as one house sauce.

Sauce for Barbecues (receipt 335)

Mrs. A. P. Hill · Mrs. Hill's New Cook-Book · 1867 · p. 171 · source · Public domain
Melt half a pound of butter; stir into it a large tablespoonful of mustard, half a teaspoonful of red pepper, one of black, salt to taste; add vinegar until the sauce has a strong acid taste. The quantity of vinegar will depend upon the strength of it. As soon as the meat becomes hot, begin to baste, and continue basting frequently until it is done; pour over the meat any sauce that remains.

Mustard, vinegar, butter and pepper, written out in 1867. The maker's own formula is not published; these are free-to-use recipes of the same family, for a cook who wants to make a jar.

Savory Mustard Sauce

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • ½ cup Dijon mustard
  • ¼ cup chicken broth
  • 1 ¼ tsp cornstarch
  • 1 pinch of salt
  • ¼ tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 shallot, very finely minced
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
1. Heat olive oil in a large slope-sided skillet over medium high heat. Add shallot and sauté until golden. 2. Meanwhile, combine broth and starch. Set aside. 3. Once shallot has cooked, add mustard, salt, and pepper to pan. Decrease heat to medium low heat. 4. Once sauce heats to just below a simmer, add broth mixture. Cook, whisking continuously, until sauce thickens slightly. 5. Whisk in butter 1 tbsp at a time, waiting for the one before it to fully melt before adding another. 6. Once all butter has been used, remove sauce from heat. 7. Serve with a steak or some pork tenderloin, or use as a grilling baste.

Not to be confused with
