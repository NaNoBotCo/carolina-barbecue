a saucelight tomato sauce

also: light-tomato barbecue sauce · vinegar-and-ketchup sauce · thin red sauce

It is 'the sauce' at the counter; the map calls it light tomato.

Vinegar-and-pepper sauce with ketchup added — enough to redden and sweeten it a little, not enough to thicken it — and often cayenne or a bottled hot sauce. The state tourism office's description is the whole recipe: 'a vinegar-and-pepper sauce with ketchup added for a little sweetness'. It is the sauce of South Carolina's Pee Dee and upper Midlands on the state's four-sauce map, and the same idea as the Lexington dip across the line, which the South Carolina Barbeque Association counts as the same sauce. It goes on chopped pork at the block and stands on the table.

The story Cited · web-scba-bbq-history

The South Carolina Barbeque Association dates the light tomato sauce to about 1900, 'vinegar and pepper with tomato ketchup added'; Wikipedia ties it to the affordability of mass-produced ketchup in the early twentieth century and to North Carolina's influence. The barbecue-sauce article's timeline agrees: sugar, ketchup and Worcestershire entered sauces in the 1920s. Kovacik and Winberry's 1987 geography of the state drew the four regions the tourism office still publishes, and Robert Moss answers that there are really two sauces, mustard and vinegar, of which this is a variant. Inference — the sauce is the vinegar tradition's own sweetening, made in the same kitchens that kept the older sauce, which is why on the map it shares the Pee Dee with vinegar-and-pepper rather than displacing it.

How it is done Tradition

Cider vinegar, salt, black pepper, crushed red pepper; ketchup by the spoon until it colors; water to thin; cayenne or hot sauce; sugar if the house likes. Warmed, not cooked down. On the block it goes into the chopped meat; on the table it is the second bottle beside vinegar-and-pepper or mustard.

Today Cited · web-texas-pete-eastern-carolina

On the table at Pee Dee and upper-Midlands pits under the house name; Texas Pete's bottled Eastern Carolina BBQ Sauce — apple cider vinegar, tomato paste, red pepper flakes, sugar — is, by this map's terms, a light tomato sauce.

Eastern North Carolina Barbecue Sauce

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • ¾ gallons apple cider vinegar
  • ⅛ cup salt
  • ¼ cup ground black pepper
  • ¼ cup red pepper flakes
  • 1 cup molasses
  • 2 cups honey
1. Start with apple cider vinegar in saucepan over medium heat. 2. Add salt, ground black pepper, crushed red pepper, molasses, and honey. 3. Let mixture heat up, but not quite boil. 4. Mix well. 5. Return sauce to the jug that the vinegar came in to cool.

Makes ¾ gallon.

The vinegar sauce before any ketchup goes in; a light-tomato sauce is this with enough ketchup to redden it.

Barbecue Sauce (Sweet)

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • 1 cup tomato catsup (ketchup)
  • ¾ cup cider vinegar
  • 2–3 tablespoons mustard
  • 3 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 1–2 teaspoons celery seed
  • 3 tablespoons margarine
  • ¼ teaspoon black pepper
  • ¼ teaspoon red pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon onion flakes
  • Garlic powder or minced garlic to taste (optional)
1. Combine all ingredients together and bring to a boil. Reduce heat and simmer 10 to 15 minutes, until it thickens slightly. 2. Arrange chicken pieces or pork chops in a greased baking dish. 3. Pour half of sauce over and bake at 350°F (175°C) for 30 to 40 minutes. 4. Pour remaining sauce over before serving.

A cup of ketchup to three quarters of a cup of cider vinegar: heavier than a Pee Dee light-tomato sauce, lighter than a Kansas City one.

Sauce for Barbecues (receipt 335)

Mrs. A. P. Hill · Mrs. Hill's New Cook-Book · 1867 · p. 171 · source · Public domain
Melt half a pound of butter; stir into it a large tablespoonful of mustard, half a teaspoonful of red pepper, one of black, salt to taste; add vinegar until the sauce has a strong acid taste. The quantity of vinegar will depend upon the strength of it. As soon as the meat becomes hot, begin to baste, and continue basting frequently until it is done; pour over the meat any sauce that remains.

The vinegar mop the tomato was later added to.

Not to be confused with

  • Lexington dip — The same sauce; the state line is the tell, and the slaw — red slaw beside it means Lexington.
  • heavy tomato sauce — Light tomato pours and soaks in; heavy tomato coats.
