a sauceSam Jones BBQ sauce

also: Sam Jones Sweet BBQ Sauce · Sam Jones Vinegar

The sauce from the Ayden family's Winterville restaurant.

A bottled line from the whole-hog house Sam Jones opened at Winterville in 2015, third generation of the Skylight Inn family at Ayden. The Sweet bottle's Nutrition Facts give 10 grams of total sugars and 210 milligrams of sodium in a 2-tablespoon serving, which is 5 grams of sugar and 105 milligrams of sodium to the tablespoon, but the record carries no ingredient list, so its order cannot be read and its base is recorded as other.

The story Tradition

The Jones family cooks whole hogs over wood at Ayden and has since Pete Jones opened the Skylight Inn in 1947, in a line the family traces to Skilton Dennis in 1830. The sauce a whole-hog pit of that kind puts on chopped pork is vinegar, pepper and salt, mixed by the bucket at the block.

Inference — the bottle is a separate object from the bucket. A sweet sauce at five grams of sugar to the tablespoon is not what goes on the meat at the pit; it is what a customer takes home, and what ships. The distance between a pit's own sauce and the pit's bottled sauce is one of the things this project can only measure on one side.

Eastern North Carolina Barbecue Sauce

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • ¾ gallons apple cider vinegar
  • ⅛ cup salt
  • ¼ cup ground black pepper
  • ¼ cup red pepper flakes
  • 1 cup molasses
  • 2 cups honey
1. Start with apple cider vinegar in saucepan over medium heat. 2. Add salt, ground black pepper, crushed red pepper, molasses, and honey. 3. Let mixture heat up, but not quite boil. 4. Mix well. 5. Return sauce to the jug that the vinegar came in to cool.

Makes ¾ gallon.

The vinegar sauce of the county the Jones family cooks in, freely licensed. The maker's own formula is not published; these are free-to-use recipes of the same family, for a cook who wants to make a jar.

Eastern North Carolina Barbecued Pig

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • 80 pound pig (butchered with head removed)
  • Salt
  • 40 pounds charcoal (for charcoal cooking option)
  • Your favorite vinegar-based Eastern NC sauce
1. Salt hams, ribs/midsection and shoulders liberally and place skin side up on either a gas or charcoal grill or a BBQ pit. 2. Keep lid closed, and aim for a heat of approximately 275–350°F. 3. Allow 1 hour per 10 pounds cooking time (gas or charcoal). 4. 2 hours before being done, place pig with the skin side down. As soon as it is flipped, pour BBQ sauce on meat side. 5. Close lid and allow skin to crisp. 6. The pig will be done to perfection when the ham bone can be twisted freely. 7. Lift lid and allow guests to pick/pull meat, or the chef can remove meat and chop to their liking on a chopping block.

Makes about 27 lb of meat, serving 30–40.

The whole hog the sauce is made for, at a size a home pit can manage.

Barbecue Sauce (Sweet)

Wikibooks Cookbook · read 16 September 2026 · source · CC BY-SA 4.0
  • 1 cup tomato catsup (ketchup)
  • ¾ cup cider vinegar
  • 2–3 tablespoons mustard
  • 3 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 1–2 teaspoons celery seed
  • 3 tablespoons margarine
  • ¼ teaspoon black pepper
  • ¼ teaspoon red pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon onion flakes
  • Garlic powder or minced garlic to taste (optional)
1. Combine all ingredients together and bring to a boil. Reduce heat and simmer 10 to 15 minutes, until it thickens slightly. 2. Arrange chicken pieces or pork chops in a greased baking dish. 3. Pour half of sauce over and bake at 350°F (175°C) for 30 to 40 minutes. 4. Pour remaining sauce over before serving.

Ketchup, cider vinegar and brown sugar, for the sweet bottle's end of the range.
