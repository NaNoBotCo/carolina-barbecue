a wordchopped

also: chopped barbecue · coarse chopped · coarse chop · chop · minced · sliced

'Chopped or sliced?' is the question at a Lexington counter, and 'coarse chopped' gets it in bigger pieces. In the east nobody asks: the whole hog is chopped, and fine.

The way most Carolina barbecue reaches the tray: cooked meat cut across on a block with cleavers — in the east fine enough to hold the thin vinegar sauce, in the Piedmont in larger pieces. At a Lexington counter it is an ordering word with a scale, chopped, coarse chopped, sliced, and each is a different plate from the same shoulder. 'Sliced' is the far end, the shoulder cut into slabs and not chopped at all. A whole hog is chopped all together, skin included, so that every tray gets every part.

Root. chop (v.), mid-14c., 'to cut with a quick blow', of uncertain origin, perhaps Old North French choper or Old French couper 'to cut'; the noun 'a slice of mutton, lamb or pork' from the 1630s. Coarse chopped: the same, in larger pieces. Minced: Old French mincier 'to chop in little pieces', in English cooking from the late 14c. Sliced: the far pole — not chopped at all
First seen. chop, verb, mid-14c. (etymonline); the counter sense is undated in the sources consulted

The story Cited · ncpedia-barbecue

Chop is a Middle English verb of uncertain parentage, and by the 1630s a chop was a slice of pork. The barbecue sense grows out of the whole hog: once the meat comes off the bones it goes to the block, and the food writers Jane and Michael Stern called the sound of cleavers on it among the most alluring things about Carolina whole hog. NCpedia gives the eastern meat as 'boned, chopped, or shredded' and the western as 'chopped or sliced pork shoulders or butts'; the Wikipedia article has the east chopping after the meat is pulled from the bone, for a finer texture that holds the thin sauce, and Lexington serving 'pulled' or very lightly chopped, leaving strands. Melton's in Rocky Mount chopped whole hog so fine that a 1988 magazine compared it to chopped peanut butter. At Lexington Barbecue the menu on 15 September 2026 reads Chopped BBQ, Coarse Chopped BBQ and Sliced BBQ, on plate, tray or sandwich, a chopped plate at $13.40 cash. Tradition holds — some eastern pits call their finest chop minced. Inference — the scale exists at Lexington because a shoulder can be sliced to order and a whole hog cannot.
