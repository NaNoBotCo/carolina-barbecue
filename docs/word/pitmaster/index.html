a wordpitmaster

also: pit master · pit-master · pit man · pit boss

Press and menus say pitmaster. Older Carolina speech says the cook, or the pit man, or the man's name — a cook forty years at the pit is called by his first name before either.

The person who runs the pit — the fire, the coals, the turning of the hog, the call that it is done. A recent word for an old job. Wiktionary defines it as one who operates a barbecue pit and adds that it is used as a term of respect; dictionary.com dates it 1980–85; Wikipedia titles its articles 'Ed Mitchell (pitmaster)' and 'Rodney Scott (pitmaster)', and BBQ Pitmasters has been on cable since 2009. In the Carolinas the older words are the cook and the pit man, and a place is known by a family's name before any title.

Root. pit (Old English pytt, the cooking pit) + master (Old English, from Latin magister 'chief, teacher'; 'a workman qualified to carry on a trade on his own account' from c. 1300), on the pattern of quartermaster and the other -master compounds for the person in charge of a thing
First seen. 'pit master', of a barbecue cook: 22 September 1939, Daily Times-News, Burlington, North Carolina — 'Jeter Crutchfield, pit master, will supervise the beginning of roasting pork for the barbecue tonight at midnight' — as quoted by Texas Monthly (2018) and Destination BBQ (2021); the one-word form 1980–85 (dictionary.com)
The compound had other lives first: an English coal-mining word in the nineteenth century, a sportswriter's word for boxing referees in the 1920s, and in the 1970s a label for snake wranglers and a cockfight ringleader (Texas Monthly). Wiktionary carries no citation; the OED was not consulted. Destination BBQ notes that Crutchfield was white and that the verb is supervise: the cooking that night was done by Black men. The story Cited · web-texas-monthly-call-barbecue-greats

Pit is the hole and master is Latin magister, the chief; the compound follows quartermaster. Texas Monthly traced 'pit master' through unrelated trades and found the earliest barbecue use it could in a 1939 North Carolina newspaper item: 'Jeter Crutchfield, pit master, will supervise the beginning of roasting pork for the barbecue tonight at midnight.' Destination BBQ gives the paper as the Burlington Daily Times-News of 22 September 1939 and points at the verb: Crutchfield supervised, and the men at the pit that night were Black. Adrian Miller's Black Smoke traces the history of those cooks — Black barbecuers, pitmasters and restaurateurs — and their place in the tradition. Dictionary.com dates the one-word pitmaster to 1980–85; the television series BBQ Pitmasters premiered on TLC on 3 December 2009. Rodney Scott of Hemingway, named Best Chef Southeast by the James Beard Foundation in 2018, was only the second pitmaster to win a Beard chef award. Inference — the word arrived with the audience: once barbecue was written about, the cook needed a title a writer could print. Tradition holds — in the Carolinas he still answers to his name.
