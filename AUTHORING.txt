CAROLINA BARBECUE — WRITING A RECORD
====================================

One JSON file per node, in data/nodes/<type>/<id>.json. The schema is
schema/node.schema.json. Copy an exemplar and change every field:

  data/nodes/style/eastern-nc.json      a style (rich: what / story / how / today, kin, confusable)
  data/nodes/term/barbecue.json         a word (etymology block)
  data/nodes/place/skylight-inn.json    a place (geo, address, links, harvested fields)

Run  python3 tools/validate.py  until your records pass. Kin targets that are
still unwritten show as errors; that is expected while the set is being built.

TYPES
  style   a regional tradition            sauce   a sauce or dip
  dish    a thing eaten                    pit     a cut, fuel, pit, cooker, tool or method
  place   a restaurant, stand, pit house   person  a pitmaster, family, writer, organizer
  org     a society, campaign, archive     event   a festival, fundraiser, political barbecue
  term    a word, with its root

FIELDS THAT CARRY THE WEIGHT
  names.name     the term as the tradition uses it. names.said = how it is said locally.
  text.what      one plain paragraph. Present tense. What it is.
  text.story     the narrative, told as narrative, where there is one. Scenes, dates,
                 names. Mark the tier of each stretch inline:
                   plain prose        = cited in this record's sources
                   *Tradition holds —*  general knowledge of the tradition, hedged
                   *Inference —*        this project's own reasoning
  text.how       how it is made, served, eaten, done. text.today = current state, dated.
  kin[]          what another node IS to this one, said from here. Each entry is one
                 sentence a reader standing on this page would want: not "see also",
                 but the fact that makes the connection ("its slaw is red because it is
                 dressed with the dip"). The other record says its own sentence back.
                 Link only to ids in the cast list below.
  etymology      required for type=term, welcome on any node whose name has a root.
                 root = the etymon chain or morpheme split; first_attested = a date you
                 can name with a source, else blank; folk etymologies go in note,
                 labelled as folk.
  facets         open keys per type, see data/vocab/facets.json. state, meat, base,
                 course, kind, role, status, founded, fuel, styles, when, letter.
  sources[]      ids from data/sources/sources.json ONLY. Add new ones to
                 data/sources/new-<yourname>.json in the same shape; they are merged.
                 Wikipedia: s:wp-<slug>. A web page: s:web-<slug> with title, publisher,
                 url, and the date you read it. A book: title, author, publisher, year —
                 never an ISBN you did not see.
  provenance     default tier + per-field overrides. Tiers: cited, harvested, tradition,
                 inference, field. A date, a founding year, a death, a court case, a
                 price: cited or it is hedged.
  geo / address  places and events. Coordinates from OSM (data/harvest/osm-places.json)
                 or the address geocoded by hand; precision says which.
  images[]       leave empty. Put candidate Commons file titles in x_commons_files:
                 ["File:Whole hog at Wilbur's Barbecue.jpg"]; tools/harvest_commons.py
                 downloads them with licences. Only CC0 / PD / CC BY / CC BY-SA / FAL.
  confidence     high / medium / low. needs_verification: true when a fact is unchecked.

WORDS
  Reader-facing text is checked by validate.py. It refuses: load-bearing, honest,
  authentic, inauthentic, purist, tourist, "real barbecue", "true barbecue".
  Describe; do not rank. Gas cookers, chain restaurants, bottled sauces and
  Texas-style pits in Charleston are part of the living tradition and are
  described the same way as a wood pit. A campaign that certifies wood pits
  is reported as a fact about the tradition, not adopted as its standard.
  No sentence that announces what the next sentence will do. No closing
  summary. Every non-obvious word defined in a clause at first use.
  Prices, when a source gives them, go in with their date.
  Stories are told as stories: the 1980s columnists' war, the Piggie Park
  case, the Lexington lineage, the 1830 wagon. Source-marked, never padded.

SECOND WAVE FIELDS (added 2026-09-16)
  tags[]          on places: {tag, source, tier?, note?, since?, url?}. Keys in
                  data/vocab/tags.json: black-owned woman-owned lgbtq-friendly
                  wood-cooked whole-hog family-run cash-only sold-out-closes buffet
                  weekends-only hash closed. EVERY tag names its evidence. Ownership
                  and welcome tags (black-owned, woman-owned, lgbtq-friendly) come only
                  from the owner's own words, a press profile that names the owner, or
                  a public directory — never from a name, a photograph or a guess. A
                  place with no evidence gets no tag; absence of a tag says nothing.
  recognitions[]  on places: {by, what, year?, source, url?}. `by` from
                  data/vocab/recognizers.json (james-beard ncbs-trail true-cue scba
                  sfa garner reed-holy-smoke fertel miller-black-smoke michelin
                  southern-living garden-and-gun our-state eater national-geographic
                  nc-legislature other). One entry per recognizer per place. The
                  count is a fact about attention paid, not a ranking by us.
  recipes[]       on dishes and sauces: {title, source, license, year?, url?, author?,
                  book?, page?, ingredients[]?, text?, yield?, note?, region?}.
                  Public-domain cookbooks (US, before 1929) may be copied in full
                  into `text`; CC BY / CC BY-SA texts too, with the source named;
                  anything else is `license: "ingredients-only"` with ingredients[]
                  and no text. Modernise nothing; keep the old spellings.
  profile         on sauces: measured from a label or a recipe: {kind: bottle|recipe
                  |house, base, maker, town, state, ingredients[] (label order),
                  tomato_rank, vinegar_rank, mustard_rank, sugar_rank (1-based
                  position, 0 absent, null unknown), sugar_g_per_tbsp, serving_tbsp,
                  sodium_mg_per_tbsp, heat, source, url, accessed, note}.
  sections[]      on art and story records: [{h, text, images?[indices]}] after
                  text.what. Same source marks as text.story.
  type art        a piece or genre: a sign, a mascot, a print, a postcard, a statue,
                  a mural, a photograph series. facets.kind from facets.json.
                  images[] are the point; text.what says what the genre is.
  type story      a long read: the wars, a map explained, resources. facets.kind
                  essay | map | quiz | resources.
  x_commons_files on any record: Commons file titles for tools/harvest_commons.py.
  x_loc_ids       on any record: loc.gov item ids for tools/harvest_loc.py
                  (public-domain / no-known-restrictions items only).

VERIFICATION CORPUS
  Plain-text copies of the Wikipedia articles the set cites sit in the scratchpad
  folder named in your brief (wiki/*.txt, each with URL and revision). Read them
  before writing; fetch others with WebFetch when a fact is not there. A fact you
  could not find is written as *Tradition holds —* or left out. Never invent a
  date, a quote, a price, a menu item or a person's death.
